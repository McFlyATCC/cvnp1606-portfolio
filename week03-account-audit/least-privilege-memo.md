ACME Access Control and Least-Privilege Memo

Purpose



This memo documents the rationale for assigning administrative privileges, identifies when escalation is appropriate, and outlines the evidence required to support temporary access exceptions in accordance with the principle of least privilege.



When Local Administrator Access Is Justified



Local administrator access should only be granted when a user's job responsibilities require the ability to perform system-level tasks. Examples include IT support technicians, desktop administrators, and other personnel responsible for installing software, managing services, configuring hardware, or troubleshooting operating system issues. Access decisions should be based on documented business requirements rather than convenience. Seasonal staff and kiosk users typically perform limited business functions and can complete their duties effectively with standard user privileges.



When to Escalate Instead of Granting Administrator Access



Requests for administrator access should be escalated when no clearly defined business need exists or when alternative solutions may be available. In many cases, a request for elevated privileges may indicate the need for software packaging, centralized deployment, policy modification, or additional technical support rather than permanent administrative rights. At ACME, escalation should be directed to the Systems Administration or Security team and include the user's role, business justification, requested task, expected duration of access, and any observed impact on operations. The expected outcome is a documented approval, denial, or alternative solution that maintains security while meeting business requirements.



Evidence Required for an Exception Request



Temporary administrator access may be appropriate for approved one-time activities such as hardware configuration, software installation, or data migration. Exception requests should include a clear business justification, a description of the specific task to be completed, the expected duration of elevated access, management approval, and documentation of any less-privileged alternatives that were evaluated. The request should also identify the audit records that will be used to verify the access was used only for its approved purpose. Maintaining this documentation supports accountability, compliance, and post-implementation review.



Conclusion



Applying least-privilege principles reduces security risk while still allowing users to perform required job functions. Administrator access should be limited to personnel with a demonstrated operational need, escalations should be used when privilege requirements are unclear, and all exception requests should be supported by appropriate business and audit documentation.
